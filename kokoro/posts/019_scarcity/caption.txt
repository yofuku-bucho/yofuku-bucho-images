「残りわずか」だと、つい手が伸びるのはなぜ？
Why "only a few left" makes us want it

同じクッキーを、10枚入りの瓶と2枚入りの瓶で出して評価してもらう実験があります。

結果は、2枚の瓶のほうが「おいしそう」「価値が高い」と評価されました。中身はまったく同じなのに。

さらに、最初は10枚あったのに途中で2枚に減った瓶は、はじめから2枚の瓶よりも欲しくなりました。特に「人気で減った」と思ったときに評価が一番高くなりました。

これが「希少性の法則」です。
「残りわずか」「ラスト1点」「今だけ」。年末のセールや福袋で、つい手が伸びるのはこのせいかもしれません。
カゴに入れる前に「本当に欲しい？」とひと呼吸。

出典: Worchel, Lee & Adewole (1975), Journal of Personality and Social Psychology

— English —
Why does "only a few left" make us want it more?

In one experiment, people rated the same cookies served from a jar of 10 or a jar of 2.

The cookies from the jar of 2 were rated as more desirable and more valuable, even though they were identical.

And when a jar went from 10 down to 2, people wanted those cookies even more than the ones that had been scarce from the start, especially when they thought others had taken them.

This is the scarcity effect.
"Almost sold out," "last one," "today only." It may be why we overbuy in year-end sales.
Before you add it to your cart, ask: do I really want this?

Source: Worchel, Lee & Adewole (1975), Journal of Personality and Social Psychology
